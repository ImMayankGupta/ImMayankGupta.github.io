## Table of contents

## 1. What is the role of a Business Analyst?

A Business Analyst is responsible for analyzing business processes, gathering requirements, and bridging the gap between business stakeholders and technical teams. They identify business needs and recommend solutions to improve efficiency, productivity, and profitability.

## 2. How do you prioritize requirements?

Assess importance and urgency based on business value, stakeholder needs, and project constraints. Common techniques:

- **MoSCoW** — Must-Have, Should-Have, Could-Have, Won't-Have
- **Kano model** — Basic, Performance, Excitement
- **Value vs Effort matrix**
- **RICE scoring** — Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort

## 3. Functional vs non-functional requirements

- **Functional** — What the system should do (features, functions, behavior)
- **Non-functional** — Quality attributes (performance, security, usability, scalability, reliability)

## 4. How do you gather requirements?

Techniques include:

- **Interviews** (1:1 with stakeholders)
- **Workshops** (group brainstorming, JAD sessions)
- **Surveys/questionnaires**
- **Observations** (shadowing users)
- **Document analysis** (existing systems, policies)
- **Prototyping**
- **Focus groups**

## 5. What is a use case?

Describes interactions between actors (users or systems) and a system to achieve specific goals. Includes:

- Actor(s)
- Preconditions
- Main flow (numbered steps)
- Alternative flows
- Postconditions

Created by identifying actors, defining goals, and documenting step-by-step interactions.

## 6. What is SWOT analysis?

**SWOT** (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats) — a technique to assess an organization's internal and external factors:

- **Strengths & Weaknesses** — Internal
- **Opportunities & Threats** — External

Helps in strategic planning and decision-making.

## 7. How do you handle scope creep?

Uncontrolled changes/additions to project requirements. Mitigation:

- Establish a robust **change management process**
- Clearly define project scope upfront (Statement of Work)
- Ensure stakeholders understand the impact on budget, timeline, and resources
- Document all change requests with business justification
- Get formal approval before implementing changes

## 8. Purpose of a Business Requirements Document (BRD)

Outlines the business needs, goals, and requirements for a project. Serves as a communication tool between stakeholders and the development team, ensuring common understanding of objectives. Typical contents:

- Business objectives
- Scope
- Functional requirements
- Non-functional requirements
- Constraints and assumptions
- Success criteria

## 9. Ensuring successful communication with stakeholders

- Active listening
- Clear and concise documentation
- Regular status updates
- Stakeholder engagement throughout the project lifecycle
- Tailor communication style to different audiences (execs vs technical teams)
- Use appropriate channels (meetings, emails, dashboards)

## 10. Handling conflicting requirements

Requires diplomacy, negotiation, and focus on project objectives:

- Facilitate discussions between conflicting parties
- Identify common ground
- Work toward consensus
- Prioritize based on business value
- Escalate to sponsor if needed

## 11. Use case diagram vs activity diagram

- **Use case diagram** — High-level view of system functionality and actors involved; shows interactions between actors and system
- **Activity diagram** — Models the flow of activities or business processes within the system; indicates sequence and dependencies

## 12. Ensuring requirements are testable

- Define clear **acceptance criteria** (e.g., using Given-When-Then)
- Use measurable metrics (e.g., "response < 2s" not "fast")
- Involve testers early
- Avoid vague or ambiguous statements
- Focus on specifying desired outcomes, not implementation

## 13. What is gap analysis?

A technique to assess the difference between the **current state** ("as-is") and the **desired future state** ("to-be") of a business process or system. Identifies gaps and defines actions to bridge them.

## 14. Handling stakeholders resistant to change

Change management techniques:

- Open communication
- Address concerns
- Involve stakeholders in decision-making
- Emphasize benefits of proposed changes
- Build trust and maintain transparency
- Provide training and support
- Highlight quick wins to build momentum

## 15. Purpose of a feasibility study

Assesses practicality and viability of a proposed project. Analyzes:

- **Technical** feasibility (can it be built?)
- **Economic** viability (is it worth building?)
- **Legal/regulatory** compliance
- **Operational** feasibility (can the org support it?)
- **Schedule** feasibility (can it be done in time?)

## 16. Ensuring requirements traceability

Establish clear links between:

- Business requirements
- System requirements
- Test cases
- Code
- Other project artifacts

Use tools like **Requirements Traceability Matrices (RTM)** or requirements management systems (JIRA, Confluence, DOORS).

## 17. What is User Acceptance Testing (UAT)?

The final phase of testing where **end-users** or business representatives validate the system against business requirements. Ensures:

- System meets user expectations
- Functionality is correct
- System performs as intended in a real-world environment

Usually the last gate before go-live.

## 18. Handling requirements that cannot be met due to technical limitations

Collaborate with technical teams to explore:

- Alternative solutions or workarounds
- Modifying the requirement
- Adjusting expectations
- Seeking stakeholder input to determine the best course of action
- Phased implementation (deliver what's possible now)

## 19. Agile vs Waterfall

- **Agile** — Iterative and incremental; flexibility, collaboration, quick feedback loops; adaptive planning; self-organizing teams; continuous improvement
- **Waterfall** — Sequential; distinct phases (requirements → design → development → testing → deployment); requires upfront planning; changes are costly late in the process

**Best fit:**

- Agile — Evolving requirements, small-to-medium teams
- Waterfall — Fixed requirements, regulated environments

## 20. Aligning business requirements with strategic goals

- Deep understanding of the organization's vision, mission, and strategic objectives
- Actively collaborate with business stakeholders
- Conduct thorough analysis
- Validate requirements against strategic goals
- Regularly re-evaluate alignment as strategy evolves
- Use frameworks like OKRs or Balanced Scorecard to link work to strategy
